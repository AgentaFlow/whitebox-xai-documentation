    Compliance frameworks¶

    WhiteBoxXAI covers five frameworks: ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act, GDPR, CCPA and the NIST AI RMF. For each, it ships a control catalog, tracks your applicability decision and evidence for every control, and generates framework-specific reports.

    What it doesn't do is just as important for an auditor to understand:

    • WhiteBoxXAI records evidence; it does not certify compliance. It shows which controls you've decided apply, which have approved evidence, and where the gaps are. Whether that evidence is sufficient is a judgement for your organization, your auditor or your regulator.
    • Readiness is a count, not a percentage. The dashboard says "18 of 25 controls evidenced", never "72% compliant", because a percentage invites a conclusion the evidence doesn't support.
    • Control titles are paraphrased summaries, not the operative text of the standard or regulation. Use the official text for audit-grade wording.
    • Other frameworks aren't covered. WhiteBoxXAI doesn't map evidence to HIPAA, SOC 2, US state AI laws or any framework not listed here.

    Where to work with them: Compliance & Evidence for controls and evidence, and Reports for framework documents.

    At a glance¶

    Framework Catalog What's covered Framework reports
    ISO/IEC 42001:2023 63 controls, 16 groups Clauses 4–10 (AI management system requirements) and all 38 Annex A controls Statement of Applicability
    EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) 28 controls, 10 groups Provider and deployer obligations per AI system Annex IV Technical Documentation, Conformity Checklist
    GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) 25 controls, 4 groups Articles most relevant to AI on personal data Art. 35 DPIA / Art. 22 Automated-Decision Record
    CCPA, as amended by the CPRA 22 controls, 5 groups Automated decision-making technology (ADMT), risk assessments and consumer rights ADMT Risk Assessment
    NIST AI RMF 1.0 19 categories, 4 functions All 19 categories across GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE and MANAGE AI RMF Profile

    The Quarterly Board Pack, Evidence Pack and Auditor Traceability Matrix span every framework you've adopted.

    ISO/IEC 42001¶

    What's in the catalog: the AI management system requirements in clauses 4–10 (context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement) and all 38 Annex A controls (A.2 to A.10), using the standard's own reference numbering.

    What WhiteBoxXAI records:

    • applicability decisions and justifications for every control, which become the Statement of Applicability
    • evidence per control, with administrator approval
    • AIMS policies and guidelines (Clause 5) with an approval step, and measurable AI objectives (Clause 6.2), both recorded from the dashboard
    • assessments you record, and a readiness view by clause with open gaps
    • a downloadable certification package (JSON) for your certification body

    Keep in mind: titles are summaries. You still need the licensed standard for audit-grade wording, and should check clause citations against your purchased copy before relying on them in a filing. WhiteBoxXAI doesn't certify you against ISO/IEC 42001; an accredited certification body does.

    EU AI Act¶

    What's in the catalog: obligations an operator holds per AI system that a governance platform can evidence, in ten groups: AI literacy and prohibited practices; risk classification; risk management and data governance; technical documentation and record-keeping; human oversight and robustness; quality management and the AI value chain; deployer obligations; conformity assessment and registration; transparency and the right to explanation; and post-market monitoring and incident reporting.

    The catalog follows Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended by the Digital AI Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744). Application dates are phased:

    Obligations Apply from
    AI literacy and prohibited practices (Articles 4 and 5) 2 February 2025
    High-risk requirements (Articles 6–27) for Annex III systems 2 December 2027
    High-risk requirements for Annex I systems 2 August 2028

    These dates appear on the compliance calendar as regulatory dates, each with its citation and the date it was last checked.

    What WhiteBoxXAI records:

    • a risk classification per AI system (unacceptable, high, limited or minimal)
    • conformity assessments and applicability decisions
    • the evidence Annex IV asks for: intended purpose, design, data governance and risk management measures, drawn from the system's register entry, audits and monitoring
    • explanations (SHAP, LIME, DiCE counterfactuals) that support transparency and the right to explanation

    Not covered: regulator, member-state and notified-body provisions, and the general-purpose AI model rules (Articles 51–56), which apply to models rather than to each AI system. Confirm current applicability for your organization before relying on the catalog in a filing.

    GDPR¶

    What's in the catalog: the articles a model-governance platform can meaningfully evidence: principles and lawfulness, transparency and data-subject rights, accountability and design (including DPIAs and Article 22 automated decision-making), and breach notification. It is not a complete GDPR compliance program.

    What WhiteBoxXAI records:

    • data subject requests, including Article 22 automated-decision requests, newest first
    • Article 30 processing records
    • a breach register
    • consent records as evidence
    • explanations of individual automated decisions

    CCPA¶

    What's in the catalog: CCPA/CPRA sections and CPPA regulations relevant to automated decision-making technology (ADMT) and the risk assessments the CPPA requires for it, plus consumer rights. It follows the Civil Code as amended effective 1 January 2026 and the CPPA's approved regulations.

    What WhiteBoxXAI records:

    • consumer requests
    • ADMT applicability decisions and risk-assessment evidence
    • fairness audits and explanations for the automated decisions in scope

    NIST AI RMF¶

    What's in the catalog: the framework's 19 categories across its four functions: GOVERN (6), MAP (5), MEASURE (4) and MANAGE (4). WhiteBoxXAI works at category level rather than the 72 subcategories, because most subcategories are organizational practices a platform can't evidence itself.

    The NIST AI RMF is voluntary. No category is mandatory, and "in scope" means you have declared it in scope, not that it's a legal requirement.

    What WhiteBoxXAI records:

    • applicability decisions per category, which become the AI RMF Profile
    • open risks per function, from the AI Risk Register
    • the measurement evidence for MEASURE: drift, fairness, explainability and LLM safety results

    One artifact, several frameworks¶

    Most evidence counts towards more than one framework. A bias audit report, for example, maps to controls in all five. File it once, and Compliance & Evidence → Controls → Evidence by artifact shows every control it supports. See Compliance & Evidence.

    From code and agents¶

    frameworks = client.compliance.list_frameworks()      # key, name, total_controls, adopted, next_audit_date
controls = client.compliance.get_framework_controls("iso_42001")   # gdpr, ccpa, eu_ai_act, nist_ai_rmf
client.compliance.iso42001_score()
client.compliance.iso42001_gaps(severity_filter="high")

    The MCP server exposes the same data (whitebox_compliance_* tools), and the compliance-evidence skill tells an AI assistant to report unassessed controls as "not yet assessed" rather than estimating a result.
